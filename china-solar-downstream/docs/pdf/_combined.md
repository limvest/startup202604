# China's Downstream Solar, 2016–2026, and What It Means for Korea

**Final report · November 2026 revision** · as of 2026-11-27 · Korean version (canonical)

## Summary

The money in downstream solar is moving from "install a lot and attach cheap capital" to "move in step with the grid". China went through this in one step when it ended guaranteed purchase prices in June 2025; Korea has entered the same path through surging curtailment and the end of its RPS in 2027.

1. **China's winners attached cheap capital to guaranteed cash flows, and that guarantee is gone.** New installations in January–September 2026 were 54% below a year earlier.
2. **Once exposed to market prices, value moved to self-consumption, storage and aggregation (VPPs).**
3. **The biggest downstream success, Sigenergy, earns 99% of revenue abroad.** Korea's equipment market is already dominated by Chinese products.
4. **Korea is meeting China's post-2025 phase, not its boom years.** The bottleneck is the grid, not demand or capital.
5. **Many Korean "opportunities" in the May 2026 edition already existed or had been tried.** Comparative work must start from the other country's current rules.

## What changed in this revision

The May edition was thorough on 35 Chinese companies but had two large problems: factual errors on Korea big enough to overturn its main recommendations, and no account of China's 2025 policy reset.

- **Corrected:** Korean programmes (solar rental programme, small-scale power brokerage, curtailment, RPS reform), housing statistics, China's 2025–2026 installations and pricing rules, and figures for 18 companies. Full log: `data/raw/verification-2026-11.yaml`.
- **Dropped:** "patterns" built on two or three cases and unsupported Korean market-size estimates (Section 7).
- **Added:** the post-Document 136 market, Australia's home-battery market, Korea's grid bottleneck and VPP rules, and a fifth segment, grid integration (VPP and storage).

## 1. Question and method

**Question:** Which downstream business models (residential installation, finance/leasing, marketplaces, O&M/monitoring) survived in China, and do their enabling conditions exist in Korea?

**Method:** profiles of 35 Chinese companies founded or entering after 2012, a policy timeline and segment analyses, compared with Korean rules. Key figures were re-checked in November 2026 against filings, prospectuses, government releases and trade press.

**Limits:** the sample over-represents visible successes; many private-company figures are self-reported; the Korean side uses public sources only. Seventeen companies could not be re-verified and keep their May figures with source grades.

## 2. Five phases of China's downstream

| Phase | Years | What happened | Legacy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subsidy boom | 2013–2017 | CNY 0.42/kWh distributed feed-in subsidy; poverty-alleviation solar | Mass entry of lessors and installers |
| 531 shock | 2018 | Sudden subsidy cut and caps | Weakly capitalised installers exit |
| Carbon pledge, whole-county rollout | 2020–2022 | 2060 neutrality; 2021 whole-county rooftop pilot | Consolidation around SOEs and large private developers |
| Volume build-out | 2023–2024 | Residential 43.84GW in 2023, down about a third in 2024 | Grid limits surface |
| Market pricing | 2025– | January 2025 distributed-PV rules; Document 136 from 1 June (market price plus partial CfD for new projects) | Rush then slump; value shifts to storage and VPPs |

China added a record 315GW (AC) in 2025, including 92.92GW in May alone as developers rushed to connect before 1 June. January–September 2026 additions were 110.83GW (-54%), and CPIA expects 180–240GW for 2026, the first decline since 2019.

## 3. Five insights

### Insight 1. Guaranteed cash flow plus cheap money decided the winners, and the guarantee ended in 2025

Leading residential players (Chint Anneng, Huaxia Financial Leasing, Yuexiu New Energy) layered low-cost capital on fixed purchase prices; the most common model paid farmers rent for their roofs. Document 136 ended that premise for new projects from 1 June 2025.

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Feb 2025 | State Grid's residential payment platform "Guang e Bao" terminated |
| May 2025 | 92.92GW installed in one month before the cut-off |
| Sep 2025 | Chint Anneng withdrew its Shanghai IPO |
| 2025 | Yuexiu's new managed capacity fell to 1.44GW from 7.47GW |
| H1 2026 | Distributed additions 42.22GW (-62.6%), C&I -77%; five provinces removed new C&I distributed PV from price protection |
| H1 2026 | Chint's residential average tariff fell from CNY 0.38 to 0.33/kWh |

**Meaning:** asset value now depends on managing price and curtailment risk, not on how cheaply the asset was financed. The May edition's top finding held for the past but missed that its condition could change.

### Insight 2. Value moved to self-consumption, storage and aggregation

- Small owners shifted to high self-consumption projects; residential was the only growing segment in January–April 2026 (+88.7%), likely because individually owned residential systems kept more protection (inference).
- Document 136 ended mandatory co-located storage; Document 114 (January 2026) introduced capacity payments for standalone storage.
- The April 2025 VPP guideline targets over 20GW by 2027 and 50GW by 2030 and recognises VPPs as market participants.
- Forecasting leader Guoneng Rixin grew revenue 30% and profit 39% in 2025 and applies power-trading tools in 19 provinces.

**Meaning:** the next winners are likely those that can move dispersed resources in step with the grid. This "grid integration" layer is now a fifth segment.

### Insight 3. Sigenergy is an export story

Founded in 2022, Sigenergy grew revenue from CNY 58m (2023) to CNY 9.0bn (2025) and raised HKD 4.4bn in an April 2026 Hong Kong IPO. 99% of 2025 revenue came from abroad (Asia-Pacific 45.9%, Europe 44.6%). It became Australia's top home-battery brand in March 2025 (25% share for 2025); Australia's rebate covering about 30% of battery cost began in July 2025. Growatt shows the same pattern: 82.3% overseas, storage 63.4% of sales, "AI energy management" 1.2%.

Korea already sits inside this structure: in 2025, 69.3% of modules and 96.1% of cells deployed were Chinese, and Chinese inverters are reported above 90%; the government aims to cut the inverter share below 60% (reported).

**Meaning:** if Korea introduces home-storage subsidies or time-of-use signals, Chinese equipment will likely capture demand first. Korean firms' room is in operations and market participation.

### Insight 4. Korea is meeting China's post-2025 phase

| Item | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Mainland curtailment days | 2 (2023) → 26 (2024) → 82 (2025) → 59 events in H1 2026 |
| Curtailed energy Jan–May 2026 | 161,805MWh, 95.3% of the 2025 total; Honam 51.3% |
| Semi-central dispatch | From March 2026; units under 20MW join via VPPs and are paid for controllable capacity; ~470MW registered |
| RPS reform | Contract market from 1 Jan 2027; new RECs end in 2026, spot market until 2029; KEPCO as 20-year buyer |
| Build-out | 3.1GW in 2025, 3.5GW forecast for 2026; targets 100GW renewables by 2030, solar 30.8GW → 87GW |

China moved from fixed to market prices; Korea moves from REC spot prices to long-term KEPCO contracts. Price risk falls, curtailment (volume) risk rises. The May edition's claim that KEPCO has a motive to block distributed solar weakens: KEPCO becomes the long-term buyer.

### Insight 5. Many May-edition Korean "opportunities" already existed

| May claim | Reality | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No residential solar leasing in Korea | Government solar rental programme ran 2013–2021, ended as subsidies expanded and certificate prices fell from KRW 211–258/kWh (2018) to 146–166 (2021) | Top recommendation already tried |
| No REC aggregation business | Small-scale power brokerage since Dec 2018; in 2019, 59,742 sub-1MW plants chose KEPCO PPAs vs 917 in brokerage (metering cost KRW 4.5–5m) | Barrier was cost, not rules |
| No domestic monitoring platform | Haezoom Home (2021), SolarOnCare (1.17GW, 8,000+ plants) | Little room |
| 65% of households live in apartments | 53.1% of households (2023); 65.8% is apartments' share of housing stock (2025) | Residential market understated |
| No state-led mass rollout tool | Sunlight Income Villages: 2,500+ by 2030; 129 applied/87 selected in round 1, 608 applied in round 2 | Village-scale aggregation exists |

## 6. Findings kept

- When government programmes replace distribution, consumer comparison marketplaces do not emerge.
- Roof-rental models generate consumer disputes (roof leaks, forced add-on sales).
- Revenue concentrated in related parties is a warning sign (Solarbao, Hanergy) — but not a rule with "no exceptions".

## 7. Claims dropped

Post-shock founding timing (three post-hoc cases); ex-Huawei founder premium (two cases); "all >50% related-party firms failed, no exceptions" (selection on failures); Skyworth "300x in four years" (unverified); Sigenergy's success being mainly AI design (99% overseas subsidy markets); Korean market-size estimates (no basis); KEPCO's blocking motive (KEPCO becomes buyer); bank-affiliated leasing as Korea's top opportunity (already tried).

## 8. To check next

Shandong timing for excluding non-individual residential systems (June 2026 vs 2027); Yuexiu's 2024 capacity definition; the Liansheng/Unisun legal entity; Qingtian's IPO; latest SOLARMAN and iSolarCloud scale; Korea's contract-market rules and aggregator capacity.

# Cross-Company Synthesis (2016–2026)

**November 2026 revision** · Korean version (canonical)

Across 35 companies, what decided outcomes was less solar technology than who captured guaranteed cash flows, and who passed risk on when the guarantee ended. Three of the May edition's six patterns are kept with stronger evidence, three are dropped, and two are added.

## 1. Where the five segments stand

| Segment | May view | November view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential | Mature, consolidating to 5–7 players | Growth over; leaders cut new builds, sell plants, go overseas |
| Finance/leasing | Mature, captured by institutional capital | New investment collapsed after Document 136 |
| Marketplaces | No consumer platform | Unchanged; installer digitalisation |
| O&M/monitoring | Platform war, AI split | OEM platforms become VPP tools; tied to hardware demand |
| Grid integration | — | New; clearest beneficiary |

## 2. Patterns kept

1. **Guaranteed cash flow + cheap capital — only while the guarantee lasts.** Even state-owned Yuexiu cut new capacity to about a fifth after Document 136.
2. **Governance warning signs.** Solarbao (75% related-party lessees) and Hanergy (60–75% related-party revenue) remain valid cases; the "no false positives" claim is dropped.
3. **What went abroad was hardware plus its app.** Sigenergy 99% and Growatt 82.3% overseas revenue; driven by foreign subsidies and high retail tariffs rather than borderless cloud software.

## 3. New patterns

4. **When guarantees end, build-and-sell transfers risk.** Jinko Power sold 729MW (652MW residential) in H1 2025; buyers require originators to keep equity and operate; the first C&I distributed-PV REIT (~130MW, CNY 320m) appeared in December 2025.
5. **Software that benefits is forecasting, trading and control, not viewing.** Guoneng Rixin grew revenue 30% and profit 39% in 2025; forecasting-device sales rose 104.6% under new observability/control rules; iSolarCloud joined European VPP dispatch.

## 4. Patterns dropped

Hardware-SaaS compounding flywheel (software revenue is small; Hoymiles fell into loss when hardware demand fell); ex-corporate founder premium (two cases); policy-timing optionality (three post-hoc cases).

## 5. Competitive map (November 2026)

| Segment | Leaders | Competing on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential | Chint Anneng (1.6m+ plants end-2024), Trina Richome (1.3m+ households, Mar 2025), Skyworth PV (29.3GW, end-2025) | Asset price/curtailment management, sales, overseas |
| Finance | Huaxia Leasing (520k plants), Yuexiu | Asset quality, operations |

# Korea Applicability Analysis

**November 2026 revision** · Korean version (canonical)

Korea resembles China's post-2025 phase rather than its 2015–2024 boom. The bottleneck is the grid, and policy has started to pay those who aggregate small resources and move them with the grid. Of the May edition's five recommendations, two had already been tried, two face changed conditions, and only one (VPP/energy management) pointed the right way.

## 1. Korea today

| Item | Status |
| --- | --- |
| New build | 3.1GW in 2025; 3.5GW forecast for 2026 |
| Fleet | 200k+ solar plants, ~32GW (June 2026) |
| Targets | 100GW renewables by 2030; solar 30.8GW → 87GW |
| Curtailment | 2 days (2023) → 26 (2024) → 82 (2025) → 59 events in H1 2026; Jan–May 2026 = 95.3% of 2025 total, Honam 51.3% |
| Equipment | Chinese modules 69.3%, cells 96.1% (2025); inverters 90%+ (reported) |
| Housing | 53.1% of households in apartments (2023); apartments 65.8% of housing stock (2025) |

## 2. Rules: opposite direction, same destination

| Item | China | Korea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | Market price + partial CfD for new projects from June 2025 | KEPCO 20-year contracts replace REC spot from 2027; weights become "preferential prices" |
| Small players | Individual residential keeps more protection | Dedicated sub-1MW contract market (e.g. Sunlight Income Villages), RESCO support |
| Control | Observability/control requirements for distributed PV | Semi-central dispatch; sub-20MW via VPPs, paid for controllable capacity |
| Aggregation | VPPs recognised (Apr 2025), 50GW by 2030 | Small-scale brokerage (2018), "integrated power plant business" licence type, VPP joint bidding in contract market |
| Storage | Capacity payments for standalone storage (Jan 2026) | Distribution-grid storage round (9 VPPs, 128MW) |

China pushed price risk onto the market; Korea reduces it through KEPCO contracts. Both see rising curtailment risk and a growing role for VPPs. The May claim that KEPCO would block distributed solar weakens: KEPCO becomes the long-term buyer, which analysts say improves bankability.

## 3. Re-rating the May recommendations

| May recommendation | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank-affiliated residential leasing | Withdrawn | Tried via the 2013–2021 rental programme; ended as subsidies expanded and certificate prices fell |
| Sigenergy-style home storage | Conditional | No home-storage subsidy or TOU signal yet; Chinese all-in-one products would likely arrive first |
| Consumer comparison marketplace | Downgraded | Relied on the 65% apartment figure (53.1% of households); no basis for market size |
| Residential REC aggregation | Withdrawn | Exists since 2018; new RECs end in 2026 |
| C&I AI energy management | Kept, sharpened | Reframed as VPP participation in curtailment, contract market and PPAs |

## 4. Open opportunities

VPP/aggregation (~470MW in semi-central dispatch, KRW 9.42/kW); distribution-grid storage (128MW connecting 182.4MW of queued solar); village and small-asset development (VPPs now fund village storage); bundled corporate PPAs (13 contracts in 2023 → 118 by May 2026).

## 5. Three lessons from China

1. Look at what survived after guarantees ended: forecasting/trading software, storage, build-and-sell.
2. Chinese equipment arrives first; localisation policy is the only lever that changes this.
3. Design consumer protection early; roof-rental models created disputes in China, and fault-repair delays were the top complaint in Korean owner interviews.

## 6. Limits

Public sources only; curtailment figures cited via an operator's summary of KPX data; inverter share is press-reported.

# Segment: Residential Installation

**November 2026 revision** · Korean (canonical)

China's residential growth phase is over: it peaked at 43.84GW in 2023, fell about a third in 2024, and 2025's 46GW was a rush before market pricing. Leaders now focus on managing owned assets, selling plants and going overseas.

| Phase | Years | Feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subsidy entry | 2013–2017 | Thousands of local installers on fixed tariffs |
| 531 selection | 2018–2019 | Weak installers exit |
| Brand entry, county rollout | 2020–2022 | Appliance brands enter; roof rental spreads |
| Volume and grid limits | 2023–2024 | 2023 peak, 2024 decline |
| Market pricing | 2025– | Rush then slowdown; IPO withdrawal, take-private, overseas |

Key facts: Chint Anneng 1.6m+ plants (end-2024), 2m (Mar 2026), IPO withdrawn, tariff CNY 0.38 → 0.33/kWh; Trina Richome 1.3m+ households (~20% share); Skyworth PV 29.3GW (end-2025), 2024 revenue CNY 20.1bn, ~5% segment margin; Zhonglai 2025 net loss CNY 1.37bn.

Judgement: dealer-network reuse delivered speed, not margin; low penetration was not the constraint — grid capacity and pricing were; the remaining edge is operating owned assets. Korea: fewer detached homes (53.1% of households in apartments) and the analogous rental programme ended in 2021.


# Segment: Finance and Leasing

**November 2026 revision** · Korean (canonical)

Bank- and state-affiliated lessors owned residential PV at scale on low-cost capital. The edge worked only while fixed tariffs guaranteed cash flow; new investment collapsed after Document 136.

Facts: Huaxia Leasing 520k plants, ~15GW, 24 provinces (end-2024), now promoting quality certification; Yuexiu new managed capacity 4.25GW (2023) → 7.47GW (2024) → 1.44GW (2025); State Grid's "Guang e Bao" terminated (Feb 2025); Hoymiles–Huarong CNY 1bn lease tie-up ended with CNY 162m used; some localities suspended filings to curb residential leasing. Counter-signal: first C&I distributed-PV REIT (~130MW, CNY 320m, Dec 2025).

Judgement: cheap capital matters only when cash flow is predictable; money moves to structures where someone keeps operating responsibility; retail-funded long-term assets are fragile (Solarbao). Korea already tried a similar model (2013–2021); from 2027 KEPCO as long-term buyer may improve financing.


# Segment: Marketplaces, Information and Installer SaaS

**November 2026 revision** · Korean (canonical)

No consumer solar comparison platform emerged in China because purchases came through government programmes and installer sales, not consumer search. The segment's reality is installer digitalisation. This conclusion is kept.

Phases: media/directories (2005–2015), S2B2C experiments (2016–2018), installer digitalisation (2019–), captive malls of large installers (2022–). Asset-management software may grow as lessors became core investors. Korea's distribution is more consumer-driven, but low purchase frequency and subsidy/village programmes make the opportunity smaller than the May edition claimed.

# Segment: Grid Integration (VPP, Storage, Forecasting, Trading) — new in November 2026

**New in November 2026** · Korean (canonical)

The May edition had no layer for moving dispersed resources with the grid. After Document 136 in China and surging curtailment in Korea, this is where money flows most clearly.

What it does: forecast output and prices; control output on grid instruction or shift it to storage; store and earn capacity payments; aggregate small resources into wholesale markets, PPAs and auctions.

China: VPP guideline (Apr 2025, 20GW by 2027, 50GW by 2030); Document 136 (market exposure, no storage mandate); Document 114 (capacity payments, Jan 2026); observability/control rules (Guoneng Rixin forecasting devices +104.6% in 2025).

Korea: small-scale brokerage (2018); Jeju real-time/reserve/renewable bidding (2024); semi-central dispatch (Mar 2026; sub-20MW via VPPs; Mar–May and Sep–Nov, 10:00–17:00; ~470MW registered, Haezoom 233MW, KRW 9.42/kW); distribution-grid storage (Jul 2026; 9 operators incl. VPP Lab, LG Energy Solution, KEPCO KDN, SK Eternix, HD Hyundai Electric, Gridwiz, EWP, KOMIPO, Hyundai E&C; 128MW/640MWh; 182.4MW queued solar connected); village storage funded by VPPs; contract market VPP joint bidding (2027).

Judgement: in both countries the share of operators who manage curtailment (volume) risk grows; competition is on forecast accuracy, dispatch compliance (Haezoom reported 96.19% in spring 2026), market access and storage assets.


# Theoretical Frameworks: How Far They Go

**November 2026 revision** · Korean version (canonical)

The May edition read the market through disruptive innovation, the S-curve and platform theory. None handles a market where policy sets demand and prices well. This revision keeps each only where it helps and makes policy discontinuity the central variable.

| Framework | Useful for | Limit | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New-market disruption | Farmers becoming plant owners | Spread was driven by tariffs and programmes, not technology | Supporting only |
| S-curve | Stage summaries | One policy can bend the curve (2025) | Replaced by policy regimes |
| Platform theory | Why no consumer marketplace emerged | Weak network effects in monitoring | Marketplace analysis only |

## New lens: who bears the risk

| Regime | Price risk | Volume (curtailment) risk | Where money goes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| China fixed tariffs (to May 2025) | Government/grid | Low | Cheap capital, volume |
| China market pricing (June 2025–) | Generators | Rising | Forecasting, trading, storage, VPPs, risk-sharing sales |
| Korea REC spot (to 2026) | Generators (REC volatility) | Rising fast | Brokerage/VPP begins |
| Korea contract market (2027–) | Reduced by KEPCO contracts | High | VPPs handling curtailment, grid storage |

Both countries point to the same conclusion: operators who manage volume risk gain share.
