# Cross-Company Synthesis (2016–2026)

**November 2026 revision** · [Korean version (canonical)](../synthesis.md)

Across 35 companies, what decided outcomes was less solar technology than who captured guaranteed cash flows, and who passed risk on when the guarantee ended. Three of the May edition's six patterns are kept with stronger evidence, three are dropped, and two are added.

## 1. Where the five segments stand

| Segment | May view | November view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential | Mature, consolidating to 5–7 players | Growth over; leaders cut new builds, sell plants, go overseas |
| Finance/leasing | Mature, captured by institutional capital | New investment collapsed after Document 136 |
| Marketplaces | No consumer platform | Unchanged; installer digitalisation |
| O&M/monitoring | Platform war, AI split | OEM platforms become VPP tools; tied to hardware demand |
| Grid integration | — | New; clearest beneficiary |

## 2. Patterns kept

1. **Guaranteed cash flow + cheap capital — only while the guarantee lasts.** Even state-owned Yuexiu cut new capacity to about a fifth after Document 136.
2. **Governance warning signs.** Solarbao (75% related-party lessees) and Hanergy (60–75% related-party revenue) remain valid cases; the "no false positives" claim is dropped.
3. **What went abroad was hardware plus its app.** Sigenergy 99% and Growatt 82.3% overseas revenue; driven by foreign subsidies and high retail tariffs rather than borderless cloud software.

## 3. New patterns

4. **When guarantees end, build-and-sell transfers risk.** Jinko Power sold 729MW (652MW residential) in H1 2025; buyers require originators to keep equity and operate; the first C&I distributed-PV REIT (~130MW, CNY 320m) appeared in December 2025.
5. **Software that benefits is forecasting, trading and control, not viewing.** Guoneng Rixin grew revenue 30% and profit 39% in 2025; forecasting-device sales rose 104.6% under new observability/control rules; iSolarCloud joined European VPP dispatch.

## 4. Patterns dropped

Hardware-SaaS compounding flywheel (software revenue is small; Hoymiles fell into loss when hardware demand fell); ex-corporate founder premium (two cases); policy-timing optionality (three post-hoc cases).
