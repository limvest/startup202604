# China's Downstream Solar, 2016–2026, and What It Means for Korea

**Final report · November 2026 revision** · as of 2026-11-27 · [Korean version (canonical)](../final-report.md)

## Summary

The money in downstream solar is moving from "install a lot and attach cheap capital" to "move in step with the grid". China went through this in one step when it ended guaranteed purchase prices in June 2025; Korea has entered the same path through surging curtailment and the end of its RPS in 2027.

1. **China's winners attached cheap capital to guaranteed cash flows, and that guarantee is gone.** New installations in January–September 2026 were 54% below a year earlier.
2. **Once exposed to market prices, value moved to self-consumption, storage and aggregation (VPPs).**
3. **The biggest downstream success, Sigenergy, earns 99% of revenue abroad.** Korea's equipment market is already dominated by Chinese products.
4. **Korea is meeting China's post-2025 phase, not its boom years.** The bottleneck is the grid, not demand or capital.
5. **Many Korean "opportunities" in the May 2026 edition already existed or had been tried.** Comparative work must start from the other country's current rules.

## What changed in this revision

The May edition was thorough on 35 Chinese companies but had two large problems: factual errors on Korea big enough to overturn its main recommendations, and no account of China's 2025 policy reset.

- **Corrected:** Korean programmes (solar rental programme, small-scale power brokerage, curtailment, RPS reform), housing statistics, China's 2025–2026 installations and pricing rules, and figures for 18 companies. Full log: `data/raw/verification-2026-11.yaml`.
- **Dropped:** "patterns" built on two or three cases and unsupported Korean market-size estimates (Section 7).
- **Added:** the post-Document 136 market, Australia's home-battery market, Korea's grid bottleneck and VPP rules, and a fifth segment, grid integration (VPP and storage).

## 1. Question and method

**Question:** Which downstream business models (residential installation, finance/leasing, marketplaces, O&M/monitoring) survived in China, and do their enabling conditions exist in Korea?

**Method:** profiles of 35 Chinese companies founded or entering after 2012, a policy timeline and segment analyses, compared with Korean rules. Key figures were re-checked in November 2026 against filings, prospectuses, government releases and trade press.

**Limits:** the sample over-represents visible successes; many private-company figures are self-reported; the Korean side uses public sources only. Seventeen companies could not be re-verified and keep their May figures with source grades.

## 2. Five phases of China's downstream

| Phase | Years | What happened | Legacy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subsidy boom | 2013–2017 | CNY 0.42/kWh distributed feed-in subsidy; poverty-alleviation solar | Mass entry of lessors and installers |
| 531 shock | 2018 | Sudden subsidy cut and caps | Weakly capitalised installers exit |
| Carbon pledge, whole-county rollout | 2020–2022 | 2060 neutrality; 2021 whole-county rooftop pilot | Consolidation around SOEs and large private developers |
| Volume build-out | 2023–2024 | Residential 43.84GW in 2023, down about a third in 2024 | Grid limits surface |
| Market pricing | 2025– | January 2025 distributed-PV rules; Document 136 from 1 June (market price plus partial CfD for new projects) | Rush then slump; value shifts to storage and VPPs |

China added a record 315GW (AC) in 2025, including 92.92GW in May alone as developers rushed to connect before 1 June. January–September 2026 additions were 110.83GW (-54%), and CPIA expects 180–240GW for 2026, the first decline since 2019.

## 3. Five insights

### Insight 1. Guaranteed cash flow plus cheap money decided the winners, and the guarantee ended in 2025

Leading residential players (Chint Anneng, Huaxia Financial Leasing, Yuexiu New Energy) layered low-cost capital on fixed purchase prices; the most common model paid farmers rent for their roofs. Document 136 ended that premise for new projects from 1 June 2025.

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Feb 2025 | State Grid's residential payment platform "Guang e Bao" terminated |
| May 2025 | 92.92GW installed in one month before the cut-off |
| Sep 2025 | Chint Anneng withdrew its Shanghai IPO |
| 2025 | Yuexiu's new managed capacity fell to 1.44GW from 7.47GW |
| H1 2026 | Distributed additions 42.22GW (-62.6%), C&I -77%; five provinces removed new C&I distributed PV from price protection |
| H1 2026 | Chint's residential average tariff fell from CNY 0.38 to 0.33/kWh |

**Meaning:** asset value now depends on managing price and curtailment risk, not on how cheaply the asset was financed. The May edition's top finding held for the past but missed that its condition could change.

### Insight 2. Value moved to self-consumption, storage and aggregation

- Small owners shifted to high self-consumption projects; residential was the only growing segment in January–April 2026 (+88.7%), likely because individually owned residential systems kept more protection (inference).
- Document 136 ended mandatory co-located storage; Document 114 (January 2026) introduced capacity payments for standalone storage.
- The April 2025 VPP guideline targets over 20GW by 2027 and 50GW by 2030 and recognises VPPs as market participants.
- Forecasting leader Guoneng Rixin grew revenue 30% and profit 39% in 2025 and applies power-trading tools in 19 provinces.

**Meaning:** the next winners are likely those that can move dispersed resources in step with the grid. This "grid integration" layer is now a fifth segment.

### Insight 3. Sigenergy is an export story

Founded in 2022, Sigenergy grew revenue from CNY 58m (2023) to CNY 9.0bn (2025) and raised HKD 4.4bn in an April 2026 Hong Kong IPO. 99% of 2025 revenue came from abroad (Asia-Pacific 45.9%, Europe 44.6%). It became Australia's top home-battery brand in March 2025 (25% share for 2025); Australia's rebate covering about 30% of battery cost began in July 2025. Growatt shows the same pattern: 82.3% overseas, storage 63.4% of sales, "AI energy management" 1.2%.

Korea already sits inside this structure: in 2025, 69.3% of modules and 96.1% of cells deployed were Chinese, and Chinese inverters are reported above 90%; the government aims to cut the inverter share below 60% (reported).

**Meaning:** if Korea introduces home-storage subsidies or time-of-use signals, Chinese equipment will likely capture demand first. Korean firms' room is in operations and market participation.

### Insight 4. Korea is meeting China's post-2025 phase

| Item | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Mainland curtailment days | 2 (2023) → 26 (2024) → 82 (2025) → 59 events in H1 2026 |
| Curtailed energy Jan–May 2026 | 161,805MWh, 95.3% of the 2025 total; Honam 51.3% |
| Semi-central dispatch | From March 2026; units under 20MW join via VPPs and are paid for controllable capacity; ~470MW registered |
| RPS reform | Contract market from 1 Jan 2027; new RECs end in 2026, spot market until 2029; KEPCO as 20-year buyer |
| Build-out | 3.1GW in 2025, 3.5GW forecast for 2026; targets 100GW renewables by 2030, solar 30.8GW → 87GW |

China moved from fixed to market prices; Korea moves from REC spot prices to long-term KEPCO contracts. Price risk falls, curtailment (volume) risk rises. The May edition's claim that KEPCO has a motive to block distributed solar weakens: KEPCO becomes the long-term buyer.

### Insight 5. Many May-edition Korean "opportunities" already existed

| May claim | Reality | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No residential solar leasing in Korea | Government solar rental programme ran 2013–2021, ended as subsidies expanded and certificate prices fell from KRW 211–258/kWh (2018) to 146–166 (2021) | Top recommendation already tried |
| No REC aggregation business | Small-scale power brokerage since Dec 2018; in 2019, 59,742 sub-1MW plants chose KEPCO PPAs vs 917 in brokerage (metering cost KRW 4.5–5m) | Barrier was cost, not rules |
| No domestic monitoring platform | Haezoom Home (2021), SolarOnCare (1.17GW, 8,000+ plants) | Little room |
| 65% of households live in apartments | 53.1% of households (2023); 65.8% is apartments' share of housing stock (2025) | Residential market understated |
| No state-led mass rollout tool | Sunlight Income Villages: 2,500+ by 2030; 129 applied/87 selected in round 1, 608 applied in round 2 | Village-scale aggregation exists |

## 6. Findings kept

- When government programmes replace distribution, consumer comparison marketplaces do not emerge.
- Roof-rental models generate consumer disputes (roof leaks, forced add-on sales).
- Revenue concentrated in related parties is a warning sign (Solarbao, Hanergy) — but not a rule with "no exceptions".

## 7. Claims dropped

Post-shock founding timing (three post-hoc cases); ex-Huawei founder premium (two cases); "all >50% related-party firms failed, no exceptions" (selection on failures); Skyworth "300x in four years" (unverified); Sigenergy's success being mainly AI design (99% overseas subsidy markets); Korean market-size estimates (no basis); KEPCO's blocking motive (KEPCO becomes buyer); bank-affiliated leasing as Korea's top opportunity (already tried).
