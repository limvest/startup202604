# Theoretical Frameworks: How Far They Go

**November 2026 revision** · [Korean version (canonical)](../theoretical-frameworks.md)

The May edition read the market through disruptive innovation, the S-curve and platform theory. None handles a market where policy sets demand and prices well. This revision keeps each only where it helps and makes policy discontinuity the central variable.

| Framework | Useful for | Limit | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New-market disruption | Farmers becoming plant owners | Spread was driven by tariffs and programmes, not technology | Supporting only |
| S-curve | Stage summaries | One policy can bend the curve (2025) | Replaced by policy regimes |
| Platform theory | Why no consumer marketplace emerged | Weak network effects in monitoring | Marketplace analysis only |

## New lens: who bears the risk

| Regime | Price risk | Volume (curtailment) risk | Where money goes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| China fixed tariffs (to May 2025) | Government/grid | Low | Cheap capital, volume |
| China market pricing (June 2025–) | Generators | Rising | Forecasting, trading, storage, VPPs, risk-sharing sales |
| Korea REC spot (to 2026) | Generators (REC volatility) | Rising fast | Brokerage/VPP begins |
| Korea contract market (2027–) | Reduced by KEPCO contracts | High | VPPs handling curtailment, grid storage |

Both countries point to the same conclusion: operators who manage volume risk gain share.
